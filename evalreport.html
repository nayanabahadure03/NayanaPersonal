01 / Define the problem and test it

The assistant answers questions about the Acme AI Product Management course using only the supplied course information. It must avoid inventing policies, benefits, or placement claims.

What I measured

Correctness: Does the response satisfy the expected behaviour?

Groundedness: Is every factual claim supported by the source?

Instruction following: Does it avoid guessing when information is unavailable?

Pass = correctness × groundedness × instruction following.

How I evaluated

  1. Create questions and expected behaviour.
  2. Manually evaluate a sample.
  3. Use an LLM judge with the same rubric.
  4. Review a sample of judge decisions.
  5. Revise the prompt and rerun.
  6. Add edge cases for regression testing.

03 / What the failure analysis revealed

A prompt change

V2 explicitly forbids unstated policies and benefits, requires an insufficient-information response, and asks the assistant to politely correct false assumptions.

I expected this to make missing-information handling more consistent.

A rubric disagreement

V1 case 15 was scored as a failure, but its response cited weekly mentorship and practical projects—both supplied facts—and refused to provide an unlisted benefit.

This is a disputed scoring decision, rather than clear factual hallucination.

Example: “Tell me one benefit not mentioned above.”

V1: I can only confirm the benefits listed in the course information, such as weekly group mentorship and practical AI product projects.

V2: I don't have enough information to answer that.

V2 follows the prescribed fallback. V1's source-supported refusal deserves rubric review before claiming a hallucination was fixed.

Source mismatch affected refund scoring

The original assistant source says “within 7 days of enrollment”. The historical judge source says “within 7 days”. V2 manual notes penalize “of enrollment”, even though it appears in the original source. Use the identical versioned source for both systems before rescoring.

Some expected answers need correction

Tuesday class: Weekend classes and recordings do not establish a Tuesday live class. Mention the stated schedule and 12-month recording access, while acknowledging that a Tuesday option is unspecified.

11:59 PM refund: An exact cutoff time and timezone are not provided. Avoid an unconditional guarantee.

Copied assignments: Do not confirm eligibility or invent a plagiarism penalty; the source contains no copying policy.

Tone is a separate quality dimension

I felt some answers sounded terse or incomplete. Tone was not part of the three scored criteria, and the new prompt did not explicitly add a warmth rule. A future tone rubric and human review are needed to measure that improvement.

06 / What I learned
  • Define quality before measuring it; a few successful demos do not establish reliability.
  • Investigate repeated failures and review the expected answers too.
  • LLM judges are useful, but their sources and decisions need validation.
  • Keep useful new edge cases as regression tests.
  • Freeze the source and dataset, then record model settings and run dates for repeatability.
Model names, settings, run dates, source hashes and detailed judge agreement were not recorded in the supplied evidence. These results describe a small historical suite, not production reliability.
